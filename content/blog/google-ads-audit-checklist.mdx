An audit is only useful if it changes what you do next. This checklist is ordered by dependency: if a check in the first section fails, results in later sections cannot be trusted.

## 1. Conversion tracking

1. Are the primary conversion actions the ones that matter to the business, such as qualified leads or purchases, rather than page views or clicks?
2. Are conversions firing once per action, without duplicates from multiple tags?
3. Is the conversion value accurate, and does it reflect revenue or margin?
4. Are enhanced conversions set up where available?
5. Are offline outcomes, such as qualified lead or closed sale, imported from the CRM?

## 2. Account structure

6. Are campaigns separated by intent, such as brand, core service and competitor terms?
7. Is budget concentrated enough for each campaign to gather data, or spread thinly across too many?
8. Are brand searches separated so they do not inflate the performance of other campaigns?
9. If Performance Max is used, are brand exclusions and audience signals in place?
10. Are location and language settings correct, including "presence" versus "interest" targeting?

## 3. Search terms and targeting

11. What share of spend goes to search terms that are irrelevant to the business?
12. Is there a maintained negative keyword list, applied at the right level?
13. Are match types used deliberately, with broad match only where conversion data supports it?
14. Are there high-converting search terms that deserve their own keywords or ad groups?
15. Are audiences used for observation or targeting in a way that makes sense?

## 4. Bidding and budgets

16. Does each campaign have enough conversions for its automated bidding strategy to work?
17. Are target CPA or ROAS values based on real margins, or set arbitrarily?
18. Are campaigns limited by budget while bidding is also constrained, causing missed opportunity?
19. Have bid strategies been changed so often that learning keeps restarting?
20. Is ad scheduling justified by data?

## 5. Ads and landing pages

21. Does every ad group have relevant, current ads with headlines that answer the query?
22. Are sitelinks, callouts and other assets in place and accurate?
23. Does each landing page match the promise of the ad?
24. Do landing pages load fast on mobile and meet Google's [Core Web Vitals thresholds](https://web.dev/articles/vitals)?
25. Is the form or call to action easy to complete on a phone?

## How should you read an audit?

A useful audit ranks findings by expected impact and effort. Watch for two failure modes:

- **Too long:** fifty findings of equal weight, many cosmetic. You need the five that matter.
- **Too generic:** findings that would apply to any account. Good findings cite your search terms, your campaigns and your numbers.

| Severity | Example |
|---|---|
| Critical | Conversions double-counting, so bidding optimises toward the wrong thing |
| High | A large share of spend on irrelevant search terms |
| Medium | Missing assets or weak ad copy in some ad groups |
| Low | Naming conventions and minor settings |
