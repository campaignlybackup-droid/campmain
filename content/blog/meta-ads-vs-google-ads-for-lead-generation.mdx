The honest answer to "Meta or Google?" is that the channels do different jobs. Google captures demand that already exists. Meta creates demand by putting an offer in front of people who were not looking for it. Choosing a starting point is about which job your business needs done first.

## How do the two platforms differ?

| Factor | Google Ads (Search) | Meta ads (Facebook and Instagram) |
|---|---|---|
| How people arrive | They search with intent | They are interrupted while scrolling |
| What you target | Queries and keywords | People, interests and behaviour signals |
| Lead intent | Usually higher | Usually lower, more variable |
| Cost per click | Often higher | Often lower |
| Creative demands | Text ads and assets; landing page matters most | Video and images do most of the selling |
| Best at | Capturing existing demand | Creating new demand and scaling reach |

## When should you start with Google Ads?

- People already search for your service or product category, such as "MBA colleges in Pune" or "dental implants near me".
- Your service is needed at a specific moment, for example repairs, legal help or healthcare.
- Your budget is modest and you need the highest-intent leads first.
- Your offer is hard to show visually.

## When should you start with Meta ads?

- Your product is new or not yet searched for by name.
- The offer is visual or emotional: fashion, food, property, travel, education.
- You need volume, and your team can follow up within minutes.
- You have creative capacity to test several ads at a time.

## What matters more than the platform?

**Follow-up speed.** Meta leads in particular go cold fast. If nobody responds for a day, much of the advantage of cheaper leads is lost. Automated WhatsApp or email responses close that gap; see [WhatsApp lead qualification flows](/blog/whatsapp-lead-qualification-flow/).

**Tracking.** Both platforms optimise toward the conversions you tell them about. If you count every form fill as success, they will find more form fills. Send back qualified outcomes instead, through offline conversion import on Google and the Conversions API on Meta.

**The landing page.** The same page rarely works for both. Search visitors want the answer to their query; Meta visitors need context and proof first.

## How do the two work together?

Once both are running, they reinforce each other. Meta and YouTube create awareness; people later search for the brand or category on Google; search captures them. Judging Meta purely on last-click conversions usually undervalues it, while judging Google on brand searches can overvalue it. Look at total leads and revenue against total spend as well as platform reports.

## A simple decision rule

If you can find meaningful search volume for your offer, start with Google Search and add Meta once conversion tracking and follow-up are solid. If search volume is thin, start with Meta, invest in creative, and add Google as people start looking for you.
