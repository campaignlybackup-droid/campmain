Agency pricing pages rarely explain the incentives behind the numbers. Yet the pricing model shapes what the agency will push for, sometimes more than the people on the account. Here is how the common models work.

## The four common models

| Model | How it works | What it rewards | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fixed monthly retainer | A set fee for an agreed scope | Efficient use of time and budget | Scope creep, or the agency doing the minimum |
| Percentage of ad spend | A share of the media budget managed | Growing the budget | Pressure to spend more whether or not it pays back |
| Performance fee | Payment per lead, sale or outcome | Volume of the measured outcome | Low-quality outcomes that technically count |
| Hybrid | A base fee plus a variable element | Balance of stability and results | Complexity; check how the variable part is calculated |

Ad spend itself is almost always paid separately, ideally directly from your card or invoice account to Meta or Google, so you can always see exactly what was spent.

## Which model suits which situation?

**Fixed retainer** works well when the scope is clear: a defined set of channels, a reporting rhythm and a level of creative output. It gives both sides predictability, and the agency has no incentive to inflate spend.

**Percentage of spend** is common and easy to understand, and it scales the fee with the complexity of larger budgets. The risk is structural: the agency earns more when you spend more. That does not make it dishonest, but it does mean you should own the decision to raise budgets.

**Performance fees** sound like perfect alignment. In practice, everything depends on the definition. "Per lead" invites cheap, low-intent leads. "Per qualified lead" needs an agreed, written definition and a way to verify it. "Per sale" requires attribution both sides trust, which is hard when several channels contribute.

**Hybrids** are increasingly common: a base retainer that covers the work, plus a bonus tied to a business outcome such as revenue growth or cost per qualified lead hitting target.

## What should be in the scope?

Whatever the model, a proposal should say clearly:

- Which channels and campaign types are managed
- What creative is included, and in what volume
- Whether landing pages, tracking setup and automation are included
- The reporting format and frequency
- Who works on the account and how often you speak
- The notice period and what you keep if you leave

## Questions to ask about any quote

1. What exactly happens each month for this fee?
2. What is excluded, and what would it cost?
3. If we doubled our budget, how would the fee change, and why?
4. If results fall short, what changes?
