Most agency selection goes wrong in the same way: the decision is made on the strength of the pitch, and the pitch is the one thing an agency is reliably good at. The questions below move the conversation to the things that decide whether your budget is well spent.

## Who owns the accounts and the data?

**1. Will the ad accounts, Pixel, tag manager and analytics be in our name?**
The only acceptable answer is yes. If an agency runs your campaigns in its own accounts, you lose the history and audiences when you leave.

**2. Can we see everything you see?**
You should have direct access to every platform as well as the monthly report.

## How will results be measured?

**3. What metric will you optimise for, and how does it connect to our revenue?**
Look for an answer that mentions your margins, qualified leads or pipeline. Clicks, reach and platform-reported ROAS on their own are a warning sign.

**4. How will you check that tracking is accurate before spending more?**
A good agency audits conversion tracking first. If tracking is wrong, every optimisation after it is wrong too.

**5. What will you report, how often and in what form?**
You want numbers plus interpretation: what changed, why, and what happens next. Our article on [what an agency report should include](/blog/what-should-a-marketing-agency-report-include/) covers this in detail.

## Who does the work?

**6. Who will manage our account, and how many other accounts do they manage?**
Senior people often sell; junior people often deliver. Ask to meet the person doing the work.

**7. What happens when that person is away or leaves?**
There should be a documented process and a second person who knows the account.

## What happens first?

**8. What will you do in the first 30, 60 and 90 days?**
Expect a diagnosis before big changes. An agency that promises to rebuild everything in week one has not looked closely.

**9. What will you need from us?**
Access, decisions, creative input and fast lead follow-up are usually the constraints. A good agency tells you upfront.

## How do the economics work?

**10. How are you paid, and what does that encourage?**
Percentage-of-spend fees reward higher spend; fixed fees reward efficiency; performance fees reward whatever is measured. None is wrong, but you should know the incentive. See [how agency pricing models work](/blog/performance-marketing-agency-pricing-models/).

**11. What is not included?**
Ad spend, creative production, landing pages and tools are often extra. Get the scope in writing.

## How do we leave?

**12. What is the notice period, and what do we keep?**
You should keep accounts, data, creative you paid for and documentation. A short notice period is a sign of confidence.

## Answers that should make you walk away

| Answer | Why it is a warning |
|---|---|
| "We guarantee first-page rankings" or "guaranteed ROAS" | Nobody controls platform auctions or search results |
| "We run ads from our own account" | You lose everything when you leave |
| "Our process is proprietary, so we can't show you" | Transparency should not be optional |
| "We'll start scaling budget in week one" | Scaling before diagnosis amplifies existing problems |
| Case studies with no time period or context | Numbers without a period cannot be judged |

## The short version

Ask every shortlisted agency the same twelve questions, in writing, and compare the answers side by side. The agency whose answers are most specific, and most willing to say "it depends, and here is on what", is usually the one that will treat your budget like its own.
