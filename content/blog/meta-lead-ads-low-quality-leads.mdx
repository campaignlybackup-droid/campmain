"The leads are junk" is the most common complaint about Meta lead generation. It is usually true, and it is usually fixable, because most of the causes sit in settings and processes the advertiser controls.

## Why does it happen?

Meta lead forms open inside the app and pre-fill name, email and phone from the user's profile. That makes them fast to complete, which is the point, and also the problem: people submit without much thought, sometimes by accident, and sometimes with an old email or a number they no longer check.

On top of that, Meta's delivery system finds people likely to complete the action you optimise for. If that action is "submitted a form", it will find people who submit forms readily, not necessarily people who buy.

## The causes and the fixes

| Cause | Fix |
|---|---|
| Form is too easy | Use Meta's higher-intent form type, which adds a review step before submission |
| No qualification | Add one or two multiple-choice questions on budget, timeline or need |
| Vague or freebie offer | Make the offer specific to buyers, such as a site visit or consultation, rather than a generic download |
| Slow follow-up | Respond automatically within minutes on WhatsApp or by phone |
| Optimising for form fills | Send qualified and converted outcomes back to Meta through the Conversions API |
| Broad creative | State the price range, location or eligibility in the ad so the wrong people self-select out |

## Should you add more questions to the form?

Usually one or two. Each question reduces volume, which can raise cost per lead. The aim is to raise the share of leads worth calling, so judge the change on cost per qualified lead rather than cost per lead.

Good qualifying questions are multiple choice and easy to answer honestly: "When are you planning to buy?" with clear time ranges, or "What is your budget?" with ranges that match your offer.

## Why does follow-up speed matter so much?

Someone who submits a form is thinking about your offer at that moment. An hour later they are thinking about something else, and a day later they may not remember submitting. Automated WhatsApp replies can confirm interest, ask qualifying questions and book a call while the lead is still engaged. ## How do you teach Meta what a good lead looks like?

Use the Conversions API to send events from your CRM when a lead is qualified, and again when it converts. Meta can then optimise toward people who resemble your qualified leads rather than your form-fillers. This takes enough qualified events each week to work, so it matters more as volume grows.

## Should you switch to a landing page instead?

Sometimes. A website form adds friction, which reduces volume but often improves intent, and it gives you full control over the page and tracking. Testing both is worthwhile when lead quality is a persistent problem.

## The order to fix things

1. Respond faster, because it costs least and helps most.
2. Add one or two qualifying questions and switch to the higher-intent form type.
3. Make the offer and creative more specific.
4. Send qualified outcomes back to Meta.
