Reports are where trust between a business and its agency is won or lost. A good one tells you, in a few minutes, whether your money is working and what is being done about it. A bad one buries that answer under charts.

## What should be on the first page?

The outcomes the business cares about, against target:

| Metric | This period | Previous period | Target |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spend | | | |
| Qualified leads or purchases | | | |
| Cost per qualified result | | | |
| Revenue or pipeline attributed | | | |

The exact metrics depend on the business. A D2C brand watches orders, revenue and contribution margin; a college watches applications and admissions; a B2B firm watches meetings and pipeline.

## What should come next?

1. **What changed.** Campaigns launched or paused, creative tested, budgets moved, tracking fixed.
2. **Why results moved.** Seasonality, auction costs, creative fatigue, a tracking issue, a landing page change.
3. **What did not work.** Every account has tests that fail. A report with no failures is not a full report.
4. **What happens next.** Specific actions with owners and dates.
5. **What we need from you.** Approvals, creative, faster lead follow-up, data.

## How often should you get a report?

A short written update weekly and a fuller review monthly suits most businesses. Daily dashboards are useful for monitoring, but decisions made on one day of data are usually noise.

## Which metrics are vanity metrics?

Impressions, reach, clicks, likes and follower counts are useful for diagnosis but not as goals. Platform-reported ROAS is useful but incomplete, because each platform credits itself. Prefer metrics your finance team would recognise.

## Red flags in agency reports

- Only platform screenshots, no connection to your CRM or sales data
- Different metrics every month, chosen to look good
- No comparison with targets or previous periods
- Cost per lead reported, but never lead quality
- No mention of what failed
- You cannot access the ad accounts to check the numbers yourself

## Can you verify the numbers?

You should be able to. Ad accounts, analytics and the CRM should be in your name, with access for your team. Spend in the report should match spend in the platform. Leads in the report should match leads in your CRM.
